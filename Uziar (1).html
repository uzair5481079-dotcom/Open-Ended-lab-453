Welcome to Ancient Rome

Ancient Rome was a civilization that grew from a small agricultural community into a vast empire that dominated the Mediterranean region for centuries. Its legacy in law, language, and engineering endures today.

[Image placeholder for a map of the Roman Empire]

We will explore three of its most enduring marvels:

  • The iconic gladiatorial arena, the Colosseum.
  • The heart of daily life, the Roman Forum.
  • The architectural masterpiece, the Pantheon.

The Colosseum

The Flavian Amphitheatre, better known as the Colosseum, is an elliptical amphitheatre in the centre of the city of Rome, Italy. Capable of seating 50,000 spectators, it was used for gladiatorial contests and public spectacles.

Key Facts

  1. Construction began under Emperor Vespasian in AD 72.
  2. It was completed under his successor and heir, Titus, in AD 80.
  3. It is the largest ancient amphitheatre ever built.

The Roman Forum

The Roman Forum (Forum Romanum) was the vibrant heart of ancient Rome, a plaza surrounded by the ruins of several important government buildings. For centuries it was the center of Roman public life.

It served as:

  • A marketplace (macellum)
  • A place for elections
  • A venue for public speeches
  • The site of criminal trials

The Pantheon

The Pantheon is a former Roman temple and, since the 7th century, a Catholic church (Basilica di Santa Maria ad Martyres). It is the most well-preserved ancient Roman building and a remarkable engineering feat due to its massive, unreinforced concrete dome with a central opening (oculus).
